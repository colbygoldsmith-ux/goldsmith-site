Profile

I grew up on Long Island, New York, and headed west to UC San Diego, where I studied Business Economics and minored in Finance. Growing up a stone's throw from New York City, Wall Street had always been the obvious dream. But through some great mentors and experiences along the way, I started to realize that what interested me most was not finance itself, but the investigation, problem-solving, and advocacy behind the work.

I paid my way through college by building a business that started with handyman work and grew into estate management for high-net-worth families. One client eventually hired me to spend six months tracing assets her ex-husband had hidden during their divorce. I combed through personal and corporate records until I could piece together where the money had gone. I loved the work. Give me a complicated record and a reason to dig, and I will keep going until I understand what happened.

That instinct ultimately led me to litigation. My interest in personal injury and medical malpractice is also personal. My brother is a physician in physical medicine and rehabilitation, so his work often begins after someone has suffered a serious injury. Hearing his stories, including those involving injuries that could have been prevented, made plaintiff-side work especially meaningful to me. It is an area where digging into what happened can actually help someone whose life has been changed by it.

That path eventually brought me to law school. Today, I am a 1L at USF on a Dean's Scholarship, working toward a career in plaintiff-side litigation.
