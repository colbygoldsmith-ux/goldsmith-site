Profile

I grew up in Huntington Station, on Long Island, and went west to UC San Diego, where I studied business economics with a minor in finance and set my sights on Wall Street. The closer I got to that world, the less I wanted a career measured only in profit.

What turned me toward law, and toward personal injury and medical malpractice in particular, is personal. My brother is a physician in physical medicine and rehabilitation, the specialty that puts people back together after serious injury. His stories, and the negligence behind so many of them, showed me where I could do the most good: making sure someone answers for the harm.

I paid my own way through school by building a business that started with handyman work and grew, client by client, into estate management for high-net-worth families. One of those clients hired me for something harder: six months tracing the assets her ex-husband had hidden during their divorce, combing personal and corporate records until I could show exactly where the money went. It taught me something about myself: I'll dig through the record until the truth is undeniable. A stint alongside a litigation attorney confirmed it, and today I'm a 1L at USF on a Dean's Scholarship, working toward a career on the plaintiff's side.
